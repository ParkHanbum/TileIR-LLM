The comparison makes exact binding facts available to the compiler. The launch ABI remains 15 parameters of 8 bytes each. The fixed configuration uses BM=128 and BN=128, a 14 × 96 × 1 grid and occupancy hint 2. Each BK32 iteration performs two ordered K16 MMAs, with FP32 accumulation followed by BF16 round-to-nearest output conversion. These computation and ordering choices remain part of the comparison contract. The evidence supports a bundled shape/stride/alignment specialization; it does not isolate the contribution of any single fact or establish which compiler pass caused the measured change.

The live binding is the contract

The tested operation is BF16 Y = A × Wᵀ, with M=1,696, N=12,288 and K=2,560 on RTX 4080. The input bank is a fixed saved real layer-0 bank, not a sweep of independent layers or activation distributions.

Alignment must hold for the actual live view, not merely its allocation base. A row stride describes element spacing, not byte alignment. The supplied summary is not a complete replay manifest; unlisted launch fields, compilation settings and artifact identities must come from the qualified source and manifest rather than being guessed.

Specialization does not remove the boundary

With M=1,696 and BM=128, the final row tile has 32 valid rows and 96 masked rows. Exact dimensions may simplify a compiler’s predicates, but they do not authorize loads or stores for those masked rows. Preserve the qualified bounds and suffix guards at export and dispatch; a fixed descriptor is not permission to widen the live view.

For the same-source C64 comparator, the secondary median of the six within-round S64 reductions is 87.113025%, also favorable in 6/6 pairs. It is an observation from this pilot, not a confidence interval, independent-run reproducibility result or general performance guarantee.

The run accounts for 30 calls: 6 qualification + 6 warm + 18 timed. The measurements are validation-separated stream-event intervals, potentially including host-submission gaps. Validation between calls can perturb residency; the repeated fixed bank is not a cache-neutral study. These intervals are not device-busy time, end-to-end request latency or model timing. Desktop/GUI activity was observed at 31%, 27% and 32% at preflight, admission and recovery respectively. Those observations constrain interpretation: this was not demonstrated to be an idle, interference-free device, and the three observations are not a continuous activity trace.

What correctness evidence actually exists

The audit reports 63 full-Y comparisons in child memory, exact tail checks and suffix-guard checks. This is stronger than comparing only a selected output slice, but remains scoped to the exercised fixed bank and checked regions. The count of comparisons is not the count of independent fixtures.

No complete GPU output was saved as a raw output artifact. The full comparisons occurred in the child process's memory. Readers cannot independently repeat those tensor comparisons from this article or reconstruct their outputs from the timing summaries. The tail and suffix checks must not be generalized to every unsupported shape, view or allocation layout.

Separate native model accuracy qualification

The independently audited follow-up runs the actual C++ model path on RTX 4080 with Qwen3.5-4B BF16, B32/P53/G16 and greedy generation. OFF and ON use the same native binary; only the prefill/qkvz export selection changes. The candidate handles 24 recurrent-layer prefill QKVZ calls. These are accuracy results, not latency measurements or a new native/vLLM ratio.

Real caller, not the standalone output allocation

The native Y extent is 41,680,896 bytes, without the standalone experiment's padding. The first actual consumer is convolution, before the BA stage. The integration therefore has to preserve the native view extent, packed Q/K/V/Z layout and consumer ordering rather than importing the standalone allocation as a runtime assumption.

Keep the earlier failed run in the evidence trail

An earlier, unchanged failed-run record reports OFF exit 4, launcher exit 1 and recovery exit 0. The first A guard ran before the normal input upload completed, after Y allocation/fill; execution stopped before QKVZ, and ON never ran. That failed attempt is not evidence of a candidate QKVZ mismatch, nor is it relabeled as passing.

failed instrumentation order:
  Y allocation/fill → A precheck → original upload completion

corrected instrumentation order:
  Y allocation/fill → original upload completion → A precheck

The repair moves only the prechecks behind the original upload-completion boundary. It adds no synchronization and leaves the default path unchanged. The later successful qualification is a separate run with this corrected observation order. This distinction matters: an invariant measured before its input is ready does not test the intended kernel contract.

No inference-speed number was measured by this follow-up. The standalone event samples above and the historical matched model checkpoint keep their original scopes.

Next boundary: broader correctness before a new timing checkpoint

The native follow-up qualifies this tested caller and fixture. Retain the live-view gates and extend coverage for unsupported views, lifetimes and error behavior before generalizing beyond it. Whole-model timing remains deferred until cumulative improvements are material enough to justify the owner’s next matched checkpoint; correctness qualification alone does not automatically trigger that benchmark. This standalone improvement cannot be multiplied across layers or combined with earlier gains as if the resulting model performance had been measured.
