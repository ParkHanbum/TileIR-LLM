2026-11-14 / experiment E07 · prepared launch plans

Prepare the invariant launch metadata once.

Caching immutable launch metadata reduced the measured native warm request median by 2.376%. A useful but modest improvement, not a resolution of the runtime gap.

Approximately 117.525 ms less per request: 2.376% reduction, 1.0243× speedup. The reduction uses the underlying pooled values; subtracting the displayed rounded medians gives 117.526 ms. This is a native-only comparison. vLLM was not rerun.

Move immutable work out of the request

The candidate prepares immutable launch metadata once and reuses it. Generation no longer reconstructs launch keys/templates through ostringstream or repeats the invariant shape construction. Startup prepares 71 plans/keys across 65 modules; generation records zero key/template builds.

Startup:     prepare immutable launch metadata → cache 71 plans/keys
Each request: bind current owners and offsets
              → check dynamic ownership / offset / ABI
              → dispatch with existing guards and fences

Unchanged: host -O3 / no NDEBUG, numerical CUBINs,
           dynamic validation, guards and completion fences

The cache does not make allocation owners or offsets immutable. Those request-dependent properties still undergo validation. Preparing a launch is not permission to reuse stale or foreign ownership, bypass ABI checks, or remove synchronization.

Raw per-request distributions and a statistical confidence interval are not included in this summary. The measured small improvement should not be generalized to other prompts, batch sizes or longer contexts without fresh testing.

Correctness includes rebinding

  • The performance experiment checked 16,384 generated token IDs exactly.
  • A separate first-three-request check matched 1,536 IDs and 1,024 down-projection hashes. This is a separate correctness test, not extra timing samples.
  • The KV boundary transition from 64 to 65 was tested.
  • The Driver qualifier exercised rebinding and rejection of stale and foreign ownership.
  • Actual exit statuses and independent recovery checks passed.

These checks support the tested ownership transitions and fixed workload. They do not establish correctness for every prompt, shape or lifetime pattern.

What remains

The result isolates one source of repeated host preparation. It does not show that all host work is gone, and it is not evidence of broad scaling across prompts. The roughly 4.83-second candidate request still leaves much larger execution-flow questions to investigate.

Higher-impact experiments should continue to isolate allocation and retirement, synchronization and guard completion boundaries, and differences in the actual runtime call flow. Those are investigation priorities, not proven causes or promised gains. The earlier diagnostic trace was a different, older configuration and was not a paired trace of these arms; it cannot attribute this 117.525 ms change.

No new native-vLLM ratio: vLLM was not rerun. Do not combine this median with a historical reference time or compound separate A/B speedups into an alleged measured end-to-end gain.
