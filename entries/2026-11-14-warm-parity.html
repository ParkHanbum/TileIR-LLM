Setup

RTX 4080, matching Qwen3.5-4B checkpoint, BF16, B32/P53/G16, greedy. N,V,V,N serial process blocks; 2 warm + 6 timed requests per block. Weights resident, prefix cache disabled, initialization and final shutdown outside the timer. See the full timer contract.

All 32 requests, including warmups, produced 16,384 IDs exact against the reference. All children and launcher exited 0; independent cleanup and lock recovery passed.

Important asymmetry

vLLM's actual CUDA Graph mode was FULL_AND_PIECEWISE. Native had no graph and retained explicit finite/bounds/canary validation. This compares the measured native runtime as implemented with optimized vLLM, not two engines with identical guard or VRAM-reservation policies.

What we learned

The runtime was not near parity. Selected standalone GEMM results cannot explain this whole-request ratio. The native path had hundreds of thousands of small guard readbacks and many allocation/retirement/synchronization boundaries, but their individual time contributions had not yet been measured.

Hypothesis, not conclusion: runtime overhead is a leading explanation for much of the gap. We have not established that all other kernels, launch counts, or device computation costs are equivalent.

Historical control is not this measurement

An earlier Python adapter measured 853–859 ms against approximately 491 ms vLLM. Disabling only vLLM CUDA Graph in a single historical process changed 490.95 to 496.34 ms. That is an adapter-era control, not the graph effect in this native runtime and not a substitute for the present result.

Evidence and next step
